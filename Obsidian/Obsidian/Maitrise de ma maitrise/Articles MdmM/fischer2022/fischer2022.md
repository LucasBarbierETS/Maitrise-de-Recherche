- source sonore      
	-  the noise sources produced by the rotating blades can be decomposed into two main categories: inflow noise, which corresponds to the interaction between the incoming turbulent flow and the blades, and self noise, which is scattered by the unsteady pressure around the blades [3, 4]. (page [2](ann.desktopURI))   
	-  More particularly, self noise includes tip vortex noise from the blades [5] and trailing vortex sheet noise [6]. (page [2](ann.desktopURI))   
	-  For high Mach number flows, steady loading noise, created by the steady forces around the blade, and thickness noise, related to the displacement of fluid by a moving object, can become important. (page [2](ann.desktopURI))   
	-  In the case of a ducted rotor, the boundary layer developing along the inner duct wall can interact with the blade tip and create either broadband and/or tonal noise depending on the nature of the eddies present in the boundary layer [7]. (page [2](ann.desktopURI))   
	-  the case of a finite duct adds phenomena such as reflection from the exit sections and scattering of sound due to the duct edges which make the problem more complex, even for a simple monopole source. (page [2](ann.desktopURI))   
	-  The interest in finite ducts started in 1973, when Doak [10, 11] proposed a mathematical model to describe the acoustic propagation inside a finite length hard-walled duct without flow. (page [2](ann.desktopURI))
