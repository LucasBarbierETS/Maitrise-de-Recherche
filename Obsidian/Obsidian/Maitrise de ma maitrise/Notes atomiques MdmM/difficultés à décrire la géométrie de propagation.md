

###### [[fischer2022]]

- « the case of a finite duct adds phenomena such as **reflection from the exit sections** and **scattering of sound due to the duct edges** which make the problem more complex, even for a simple monopole source. » (Fischer et al., 2022, p. 2)
  
 -  ![[image-3-x63-y71.png]]

- « The problem becomes even more complicated when considering sources such as **rotating dipoles**, as is the case for an in-duct fan, or **quadrupole sources** which appear when turbulence is present in the flow. » (Fischer et al., 2022, p. 2)

- « The interest in finite ducts started in 1973, when Doak [10, 11] proposed a mathematical model to describe the acoustic propagation inside a finite length hard-walled duct without flow. The effect of duct length is **expressed in terms of modal aperture coefficients** from which modal reflection coefficients, modal termination impedances and other quantities can be derived. » (Fischer et al., 2022, p. 2)

- « More recently, a different approach was proposed by Tomko et al. [2] based on an image source formulation. The method consists of using the solution for an infinite duct and adding more sources at the theoretical image source locations. » (Fischer et al., 2022, p. 3)

- « However, that method requires to know the spectra content of the rotating dipole source. » (Fischer et al., 2022, p. 3)
